# Step 6 — Report

**Goal:** get the information in front of me fast, every morning, without me having to go looking for it — and stop there. This step is the boundary between what the system does and what I still have to do myself.

## What gets published

A dashboard, updated in place every run, holding:

- Today's stats: postings screened, recommended, skipped-with-reason, blocked-as-duplicate
- Every recommended and backup pick, each carrying its Fit%, ATS%, verdict banner, and (below 70 ATS) its tweak points
- A follow-up-window section — postings first seen 5-10 days ago that are recommended, as a conditional nudge ("if you applied here, this is a good window to follow up") since the system has no way to know whether I actually applied
- A collapsible section for postings skipped with a real gap, so the reasoning stays auditable rather than disappearing
- A footer noting exactly which sources ran successfully that day and which didn't

## What gets sent to my phone

One short push notification — date, sources checked, recommended count and top picks with their scores, how many were flagged stale or grouped as volume-posters, how many are in the follow-up window. Full detail stays in the dashboard; the phone gets under 10 lines.

## The hard boundary, restated

This step is also where the system's job ends. It never submits an application on my behalf, never edits my resume, and never suggests a skill I haven't actually used, however good a match it would make on paper. Everything from here — deciding what to apply to, tailoring anything, reaching out to anyone, showing up to the interview — is mine, on purpose. Automating the search was never meant to automate the judgment.
