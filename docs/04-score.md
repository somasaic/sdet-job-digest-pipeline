# Step 4 — Score

**Goal:** answer two different questions separately, instead of collapsing them into one number that hides which one is actually true.

## Two independent scores

- **Fit %** — the holistic judgment call: skills, seniority, domain, and my location/work-mode/compensation/contract-type policy, all weighed together after the Step 3 gap classification. Only postings scoring roughly 65%+ get recommended at all.
- **ATS %** — a narrower, stricter number: how many of the JD's named tools/skills/keywords appear in my resume (exact match or clear synonym), weighted toward must-have requirements over nice-to-haves. This estimates what an actual applicant-tracking-system parser would score, independent of whether the role is actually a good fit for me.

These are **deliberately never merged.** A posting can be a high ATS match and a poor real fit (great keyword overlap, wrong location or seniority), or the reverse (a great real fit that scores lower on ATS purely because of resume phrasing). Reporting one blended number would hide exactly the information that matters for deciding what to do next — apply as-is, tweak the resume first, or skip.

## What the ATS score turns into: a tweak-point list, with a hard rule

Any posting scoring below 70 on ATS gets 2-4 concrete tweak points — specific resume additions or rephrasings that would plausibly close the gap, each grounded in an actual keyword difference found between the JD and my resume.

The rule I hold firm on: if a tweak point would require adding a skill or tool I don't actually have hands-on experience with, it gets an explicit caveat — add only if you can genuinely back this up in an interview. If a JD needs a keyword I can't honestly claim, I skip that JD rather than pad the resume. This is the one rule I will not relax for a better match rate.

## The "Best Bet" tag — added after 15 days of watching the two scores diverge

Once enough real data existed to see how often Fit% and ATS% actually agreed versus diverged, I added one small additive rule: any posting where **both** scores are independently 75+ gets a "Best Bet" flag layered on top of everything else, without changing either underlying number. It's a fast visual signal for "both independent measures agree this is strong" — not a replacement for the two-number system, just a way to surface the handful of postings where they line up.

## Result, 15 days

- 63 postings cleared the recommend bar, averaging **76% Fit / 72% ATS**
- 9 of those would already have qualified as "Best Bet" (both scores 75+) under the rule above, applied retroactively
