# Step 3 — Classify

**Goal:** tell the difference between a JD I genuinely don't match and a JD that just uses different words for something I already do — without a blind keyword scan flattening that distinction.

## The three buckets

Every requirement gap gets sorted into exactly one of three categories:

- **Real gap** — the role's actual day-to-day function needs something I don't have. Examples: Java/Selenium as the *primary* framework (not "and/or Playwright"), mandatory mobile/Appium as a core responsibility rather than a preferred line, a manual-testing-first role, or a hard seniority floor well above my level (5-8 years, or a Lead/Director title). Real gaps are legitimate reasons to skip a posting or mark it backup-only.
- **Phrasing gap** — the JD names a specific tool as shorthand for a category I already cover. "Selenium" listed as "Selenium and/or Playwright," or "Cucumber/BDD" listed as preferred rather than required. Never treated as disqualifying.
- **Stretch** — a nice-to-have mentioned once among many requirements, not load-bearing for the actual role. Not disqualifying.

Logistics friction (in-office-only when I want remote/hybrid, contract vs. permanent, unusual timezone overlap, unusually low pay) gets flagged separately from skill gaps — it's a reason to know before applying, not automatically a reason to skip.

## Why this matters more than it sounds

A blind keyword-match system treats "requires Selenium" identically whether the JD means "Selenium is the only acceptable tool" or "Selenium/Playwright, either is fine." Those are completely different situations for someone whose primary framework is Playwright. Collapsing that distinction either produces false rejects (skipping roles I'm actually qualified for because a keyword scan saw "Selenium" and stopped) or false accepts (recommending roles where a real, load-bearing tool requirement got misread as a phrasing choice). The three-bucket rule exists specifically to force that judgment call to happen explicitly, every time, instead of being skipped.

## Result, 15 days

- **183 of 430 postings (43%)** filtered out with a stated, specific reason — not silently dropped, and not flattened into a single "didn't match" bucket.
- Each exclusion is auditable after the fact: I can go back and see exactly which requirement triggered a skip and whether it was judged real, phrasing, or a stretch.
